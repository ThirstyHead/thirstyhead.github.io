Searching for Truth in the Age of Maybe

April 15, 2026 • 8 Min Read

For the first thirty years of the web, the fundamental architecture of our digital lives was built on absolute certainty. A hyperlink either resolved to a 200 OK status, or it threw a 404 Not Found. A boolean was true, or it was false. The machines did exactly what we told them to do, with brutal, uncompromising exactitude.

We are now exiting the era of exactitude. As generative AI models integrate themselves into our search engines, our IDEs, and our daily workflows, we are trading deterministic logic for probabilistic reasoning. We are building the future of the web on top of machines that are remarkably good at guessing.

The Hallucination of Structure

The challenge for Web Architects and Digital Accessibility advocates is profound. Accessibility has always relied on structure. A screen reader navigates a page by interpreting the DOM tree—the strict hierarchy of headings, landmarks, and ARIA attributes that developers meticulously code.

If the underlying foundation of our data becomes fluid, probabilistic, and prone to hallucination, how do we guarantee equal access to truth?

Consider the generation of Alternative Text (alt-text) for images. For years, advocates have begged developers to manually write descriptive text for users who are blind or low-vision. Now, AI can auto-generate alt-text in milliseconds. But what happens when the AI misunderstands the context of the image? What happens when it confidently asserts a falsehood?

The concept of "truth" is no longer just a philosophical debate; it is a structural imperative for the accessible web. As we lean into the "Age of Maybe", our focus must shift from merely enforcing syntax to verifying semantics. We must design systems that allow users to interrogate the confidence interval of the data they are consuming.
